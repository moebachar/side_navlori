# Benchmark Methods for RSSI-Only WiFi Indoor Localization: 6 Reproducible Candidates

## TL;DR
- Include **6 methods**: three must-cite baselines — **RADAR** (kNN/Euclidean, INFOCOM 2000), **Horus** (probabilistic/Bayesian, MobiSys 2005), and a **reproducible weighted-kNN fingerprinting variant** parameterised per Torres-Sospedra et al. (Expert Systems with Applications 2015) — plus three recent deep competitors — **CNNLoc** (SAE+1D-CNN, IEEE Access 2019), **Kim et al.'s Scalable DNN/stacked-autoencoder** (Big Data Analytics 2018), and one GNN, **IndoorGNN** (Springer LNCS/BDA 2023) or its regression-native alternative **GConvLoc** (IEICE 2023).
- All six are RSSI-only (no CSI). The two most reproducible for your setup are the WkNN baseline and CNNLoc/Kim-DNN because they have public code and were designed for sparse RSS vectors; the GNN competitor is the thinnest on code but is the current trending direction.
- Your data (sparse robot trajectory, ~50–80 APs, one floor, continuous (x,y) ground truth from lidar SLAM) needs three universal adaptations: drop building/floor classification heads, switch to (x,y) regression, and use your own labelled scans as the "radio map" rather than a dense grid survey.

## Key Findings
- The classic benchmark trio (RADAR, Horus, deterministic/weighted kNN) appears in essentially every WiFi fingerprinting paper and each is RSSI-only, low-parameter, and trivially adaptable to a sparse trajectory since they are memory-based (no dense grid required — they just match against whatever reference fingerprints exist). RADAR alone has over 8,000 citations and won the inaugural ACM SIGMOBILE Test-of-Time Paper Award (2016).
- Deep-learning competitors from 2018–2023 (CNNLoc, Kim Scalable DNN) were explicitly designed for sparse RSS vectors and have maintained open-source repos, but their published form outputs building/floor classification plus coordinate regression on the multi-building UJIIndoorLoc dataset; you would use only the coordinate-regression head.
- The single-floor, single-building, tens-of-scans nature of your data is the biggest constraint: deep nets risk overfitting with so few samples, so data augmentation (e.g., multi-output Gaussian process augmentation) or keeping the deep competitor small is advisable.
- UJIIndoorLoc (Torres-Sospedra et al., IPIN 2014) is the near-universal public benchmark — 19,937 training and 1,111 test samples, 520 APs per fingerprint (RSSI −104 to 0 dBm, undetected coded as +100), across 3 buildings of 4–5 floors covering more than 110,000 m². Tampere/TUT and Alcalá are secondary. None matches a robot-trajectory single-floor collection exactly, so report on your own dataset and optionally on a UJIIndoorLoc single-building subset for comparability.

## Details

### Baseline 1 — RADAR (deterministic kNN / nearest-neighbour in signal space)
- **Paper:** P. Bahl and V. N. Padmanabhan, "RADAR: An In-Building RF-Based User Location and Tracking System," Proc. IEEE INFOCOM 2000, vol. 2, pp. 775–784. DOI: 10.1109/INFCOM.2000.832252. (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/832252)
- **Venue quality:** INFOCOM is a top-tier networking conference. Per Microsoft Research, the paper has "over 8000 citations and [is] a recipient of the inaugural ACM SIGMOBILE Test-of-Time Paper Award in 2016" — it is the foundational RF fingerprinting reference.
- **Group:** Baseline.
- **Method summary:** Input is a vector of RSSI values from access points. Offline, empirical RSS is recorded at known reference points (and optionally modelled by signal propagation). Online, the observed RSS vector is matched to the reference with minimum Euclidean distance in signal space (k=1 nearest neighbour, or averaging k nearest); output is the (x,y) of the matched reference. No training beyond storing the fingerprint database.
- **Why it made the list:** With 8,000+ citations and a Test-of-Time award, it is the default deterministic baseline nearly every paper cites and compares against.
- **Open-source:** No canonical repo from the authors; the algorithm is a few lines (nearest-neighbour in RSS space) and appears in many public toolkits.
- **Data fit & adaptation:** Runs directly on RSSI-only data. For your trajectory, treat each ground-truth-labelled scan as a reference fingerprint; no dense grid is required. Handle missing APs with a placeholder (e.g., −100 dBm). Naturally outputs (x,y) via k-NN averaging.

### Baseline 2 — Horus (probabilistic / Bayesian)
- **Paper:** M. Youssef and A. Agrawala, "The Horus WLAN Location Determination System," Proc. 3rd Int. Conf. on Mobile Systems, Applications, and Services (MobiSys 2005), pp. 205–218. DOI: 10.1145/1067170.1067193. (https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/mobisys05/tech/full_papers/youssef/youssef.pdf)
- **Venue quality:** MobiSys is a premier mobile-systems venue; Horus is the canonical probabilistic fingerprinting baseline — described as "one of the earliest WiFi-based tracking systems and the first probabilistic scheme." Its author, Moustafa Youssef, is an IEEE and ACM Fellow.
- **Group:** Baseline.
- **Method summary:** Models the RSS at each location as a probability distribution per AP (handling signal variation and small-scale compensation) and uses maximum-likelihood/Bayesian estimation to return the most probable location; uses location clustering to cut computation. Output is a discrete location (can be interpolated to (x,y)). Requires multiple RSS samples per reference location to estimate distributions.
- **Why it made the list:** The must-cite probabilistic counterpart to RADAR; ubiquitous in comparisons.
- **Open-source:** No official repo; widely reimplemented.
- **Data fit & adaptation:** RSSI-only. The main adaptation is that a trajectory gives few samples per exact location, so you must bin trajectory scans into small spatial cells to estimate per-cell RSS histograms/Gaussians, or use a kernel/GP density. **Flag:** it assumes several samples per location, which your sparse data only weakly satisfies.

### Baseline 3 — Weighted kNN fingerprinting (reproducible Torres-Sospedra variant)
- **Paper:** J. Torres-Sospedra, R. Montoliu, S. Trilles, Ó. Belmonte, J. Huerta, "Comprehensive analysis of distance and similarity measures for Wi-Fi fingerprinting indoor positioning systems," Expert Systems with Applications, vol. 42, no. 23, pp. 9263–9278, 2015. DOI: 10.1016/j.eswa.2015.08.013. (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417415005527) Dataset companion: UJIIndoorLoc (Torres-Sospedra et al., IPIN 2014, DOI 10.1109/IPIN.2014.7275492).
- **Venue quality:** Expert Systems with Applications is a strong, high-impact journal; this paper is the reference for exact kNN variant choices and is reproducible on the public UJIIndoorLoc dataset.
- **Group:** Baseline.
- **Method summary:** Weighted k-nearest-neighbours in RSS space with tunable distance/similarity measures and four RSS data representations introduced/compared in the paper: positive, normalized, exponential, and powed (the paper reports that the exponential or powed representations beat the linear/positive representation for almost every distance measure). Output is a weighted average of neighbour (x,y) coordinates.
- **Why it made the list:** It is the modern, reproducible specification of the "standard" fingerprinting baseline used across the UJIIndoorLoc literature, letting you fix exact hyperparameters (k, distance metric, representation, missing-AP handling) for a fair comparison.
- **Open-source:** Community implementations exist; UJIIndoorLoc is public on the UCI ML repository (https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/310/ujiindoorloc).
- **Data fit & adaptation:** Best fit of the baselines. Directly RSSI-only, no dense grid, natural (x,y) regression. Recommended standard config: k around 1–10 (comprehensive comparisons in this literature commonly use Manhattan distance with k up to ~10), powed/exponential representation, and a placeholder for missing APs — UJIIndoorLoc codes undetected APs as +100, but many works instead substitute a low value such as −105/−110 dBm, which has been reported to give better ML behaviour than the +100 code.

### Competitor 1 — CNNLoc (Stacked Auto-Encoder + 1D-CNN)
- **Paper:** X. Song et al., "A Novel Convolutional Neural Network Based Indoor Localization Framework with WiFi Fingerprinting," IEEE Access, vol. 7, pp. 110698–110709, 2019. DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2933921. (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335074137) Conference precursor: "CNNLoc: Deep-Learning Based Indoor Localization with WiFi Fingerprinting," IEEE SmartWorld/UIC 2019, DOI 10.1109/SmartWorld...2019.00139.
- **Venue quality:** IEEE Access is a large peer-reviewed open-access journal; the IEEE Access paper has 200+ citations and CNNLoc is one of the most-used deep benchmarks for multi-building/floor localization ("one of the most popular models … widely used as a benchmark").
- **Group:** Competitor.
- **Method summary:** A stacked auto-encoder compresses the sparse RSS vector; a 1D-CNN then performs building classification, floor classification, and a separate positioning (coordinate-regression) model. Output includes (x,y) regression via the positioning model. Requires supervised training.
- **Reported performance:** On UJIIndoorLoc, CNNLoc reports 100% building-level and roughly 95% floor-level success rates; independent third-party reproductions report a UJIIndoorLoc positioning (coordinate) error on the order of ~11.8 m, and CNNLoc's own UTSIndoorLoc test reports ~7.60 m average positioning error with a 94.57% floor hit rate — a useful reminder that headline classification rates say little about the metre-level regression accuracy you care about.
- **Open-source:** GitHub — https://github.com/XudongSong/CNNLoc-Access (and https://github.com/XudongSong/CNNLoc); Keras with TensorFlow backend (originally Keras 2.2.2 / TensorFlow-GPU 1.10, Python 3.6). Companion dataset UTSIndoorLoc at https://github.com/XudongSong/UTSIndoorLoc-dataset. Evaluated on UJIIndoorLoc, Tampere, UTSIndoorLoc. (Note: the released code targets legacy TF1/Keras and will likely need porting to run on current TensorFlow.)
- **Data fit & adaptation:** Use only the SAE + positioning (regression) model; drop the building/floor classification. With only tens of scans you must augment training data or shrink the network to avoid overfitting. RSSI-only, no CSI.

### Competitor 2 — Kim et al. Scalable DNN (stacked auto-encoder + feed-forward classifier)
- **Paper:** K. S. Kim, S. Lee, K. Huang, "A scalable deep neural network architecture for multi-building and multi-floor indoor localization based on Wi-Fi fingerprinting," Big Data Analytics, vol. 3, no. 4, 2018. DOI: 10.1186/s41044-018-0031-2. (arXiv 1712.01990: https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01990)
- **Venue quality:** Peer-reviewed journal (SpringerOpen); this is the reference scalable-DNN/SAE baseline and is frequently reproduced.
- **Group:** Competitor.
- **Method summary:** A stacked auto-encoder reduces the 520-dimensional RSS vector; a feed-forward multi-label classifier jointly predicts building/floor/location. A single-input multi-output variant (Kim, CANDARW 2018, DOI 10.1109/CANDARW.2018.00045) adds coordinate regression.
- **Reported performance:** Per the Big Data Analytics 2018 paper, "building and floor hit rates achieved for the UJIIndoorLoc dataset are 100% and 94%, respectively, and the mean error in coordinates estimation is 6.20 m" (the earlier arXiv 1712.01990 version reported 99.82% building, 91.27% floor, and ~9.29 m positioning error).
- **Why it made the list:** Canonical, well-documented, open-source SAE baseline; used as a reference model in many later papers.
- **Open-source:** GitHub — https://github.com/kyeongsoo/indoor_localization and https://github.com/kyeongsoo/dnn-based_indoor_localization; Keras/TensorFlow. Evaluated on UJIIndoorLoc.
- **Data fit & adaptation:** Reuse the SAE for dimensionality reduction and attach a 2-output regression head; drop building classification (single building) and floor classification (single floor). Same small-sample caution as CNNLoc — consider the authors' own multi-output Gaussian process augmentation (Tang, Li, Kim, Smith; IEEE ICC Workshops 2022, arXiv 2202.01980; extended in Sensors 24(3):1026, 2024, DOI 10.3390/s24031026) which directly targets sparse RSS data and is highly relevant to a tens-of-scans trajectory.

### Competitor 3 — GNN fingerprinting: IndoorGNN (or GConvLoc)
- **IndoorGNN paper:** R. Vishwakarma, R. B. Joshi, S. Mishra, "IndoorGNN: A Graph Neural Network Based Approach for Indoor Localization Using WiFi RSSI," Big Data and Artificial Intelligence (BDA 2023), LNCS vol. 14418, pp. 150–165, Springer. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-49601-1_11. arXiv: 2312.07609 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07609). Code: https://gitlab.niser.ac.in/smlab-niser/23indoorgnn (lab GitHub org: https://github.com/smlab-niser). Evaluated on UJIIndoorLoc and MNAV. **Task: region classification** (Python/GNN). Reported result: "our IndoorGNN algorithm stood out with the highest classification accuracy of 95.8%" on UJIIndoorLoc, and it is noted to perform well even on restricted (sparse) training data — directly relevant to your setup.
- **GConvLoc paper (regression alternative):** D. Kim and Y.-J. Suh, "GConvLoc: WiFi Fingerprinting-Based Indoor Localization Using Graph Convolutional Networks," IEICE Trans. Information and Systems, vol. E106.D, no. 4, pp. 570–574, 2023. DOI: 10.1587/transinf.2022EDL8081 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/transinf/E106.D/4/E106.D_2022EDL8081/_article). **Task: (x,y) regression** on UJIIndoorLoc — "the best mean positioning error was 7.59 m when k = 23 … mean positioning errors lie between 7.5 m and 8.0 m for all k values except for k = 1"; implemented in Python 3.10 / PyTorch 1.12.1 / PyTorch-Geometric 2.1.0. **No public code released.**
- **Venue quality:** IndoorGNN is a peer-reviewed Springer LNCS chapter with an arXiv version and public code; GConvLoc is a short IEICE letter with better task-match but no code.
- **Group:** Competitor.
- **Method summary:** Build a graph where nodes are RSS fingerprints and edges encode signal similarity; a GNN aggregates neighbourhood features to predict location. IndoorGNN classifies into regions; GConvLoc regresses coordinates.
- **Why it made the list:** GNNs are the current trending direction for RSSI fingerprinting; IndoorGNN is the best-supported RSSI-only GNN with public code, and its strong performance on restricted training data maps well onto your tens-of-scans scenario.
- **Open-source:** IndoorGNN — public GitLab repo (license and last-commit date could not be independently verified because the institutional GitLab blocks automated inspection); GConvLoc — none.
- **Data fit & adaptation:** RSSI-only. If you need coordinate regression, adapt IndoorGNN's head from classification to (x,y) regression, or reimplement GConvLoc (its published PyTorch/PyG stack makes reimplementation straightforward). **Flag:** IndoorGNN's classification output must be converted before you can report metre-level error metrics.

## Recommendations
1. **Adopt all three baselines** (RADAR kNN, Horus probabilistic, and the Torres-Sospedra weighted-kNN variant). They are cheap, RSSI-only, memory-based, and impose no dense-grid requirement, so they run on your trajectory with only missing-AP handling and (x,y) averaging. Fix the WkNN config explicitly (k, distance metric, RSS representation, missing-AP placeholder) so the comparison is reproducible; the Torres-Sospedra ESWA 2015 spec is the cleanest to cite for these choices.
2. **Adopt CNNLoc and Kim's Scalable DNN as the two primary deep competitors** — both have maintained public repos, are RSSI-only, and are widely used benchmarks (CNNLoc 200+ citations; Kim's DNN a canonical SAE reference). Use only their coordinate-regression paths; drop building/floor classification for your single-floor data. Budget time to port CNNLoc off legacy TF1/Keras.
3. **Add one GNN competitor: IndoorGNN** (public code, peer-reviewed, strong on sparse training data) as the trending-SOTA representative, adapting its head to regression; cite/reimplement **GConvLoc** as the regression-native GNN (best mean error 7.59 m on UJIIndoorLoc) if reviewers require exact task-match.
4. **Mitigate small-sample risk** for all deep nets with data augmentation (the Kim-group multi-output Gaussian process augmentation, Sensors 2024, is a natural fit for tens of scans) and keep networks small; report both median and mean localization error in metres plus room-level accuracy.
5. **Report on your own robot dataset primarily, and optionally on a UJIIndoorLoc single-building subset** for external comparability — but expect your single-floor, ~50–80-AP numbers to be far better than UJIIndoorLoc's ~6–12 m figures, which span 3 buildings and 520 APs.
- **Thresholds that change the plan:** if a deep competitor's median error is not clearly better than the WkNN baseline on your data, keep it only as a reported negative result; if you have fewer than ~30 scans per model to train on, prefer the three baselines + one small deep net over three deep nets.

## Caveats
- RADAR and Horus have no canonical author code; you (or a well-cited third-party implementation) must implement them, introducing minor variant risk — state your exact variant and parameters.
- CNNLoc and Kim-DNN were built for multi-building/multi-floor classification on larger surveys; their headline metrics (building/floor hit rate) are irrelevant to you, their coordinate-regression errors on UJIIndoorLoc (~6–12 m) reflect a much larger/harder environment, and their small-sample behaviour on tens of scans is untested — augmentation is essentially required.
- IndoorGNN's repo license and maintenance status could not be independently verified, and its published task is classification, not regression; GConvLoc has no code at all and would need reimplementation from its PyTorch/PyG description.
- No public dataset exactly matches a single-floor robot-trajectory RSSI collection (UJIIndoorLoc, Tampere/TUT, and Alcalá are all survey- or crowdsourced), so cross-paper numbers are not directly comparable to yours; treat UJIIndoorLoc results as indicative only.
- Citation counts fluctuate; the figures here (RADAR >8,000; CNNLoc 200+; UJIIndoorLoc ~568) were checked mid-September 2026 and should be re-verified on Google Scholar at submission time.
