Radio Flow SLAM: results

All four phases of the plan were run on the 12 golden runs. In short, WiFi alone did not beat the best earlier method, and the network adds only a little. With wheel odometry, WiFi can find and remove a gyro fault that breaks the odometry, but the robot's own wheel encoders fix that fault better.

2026-10-01 · 12 golden runs, 370 scans, 24 APs · weights chosen on synthetic sites only · code in side_navlori/dpro/rf
In short
  • WiFi alone: a tie. The new solver reaches 1.47 m, the same as the best earlier method (DPRO v1 with the network off, tuned). Even with the true AP map it only reaches 1.20 m. On these runs, WiFi alone looks close to its limit.
  • Recognising a place seen before: better ranking, still mostly wrong. The learned check ranks pairs of scans better than the raw comparison (score 0.87 vs 0.78), but only 30–32 % of its proposals are really within 3 m. The best possible at that number of proposals is 61 %.
  • The learned update: real on synthetic sites, small on real runs. On new synthetic sites it beats the hand-set weights on 21–25 of 30 sites. On the real runs it gives 1.46 m (all 3 trainings within ±0.01 m) and 10 % less drift. The bar I set was 0.2 m better, so it fails.
  • With odometry, WiFi works as a check, not as a sensor. Mixing WiFi into the odometry changes nothing (0.48 m both). But in runs 4 and 9 the gyro has a constant bias and the odometry path curls into loops. WiFi alone finds that bias (run 4: −22.4 °/s found, −21.9 true) and fixes run 4 (2.55 → 0.34 m) without touching the healthy runs. Average: 0.48 → 0.30 m, after the run only. But the fault is in the gyro, not the wheels: odometry from the wheel encoders alone reaches 0.13 m without WiFi.
  • Next step I suggest: fix the odometry baseline first (encoders checked against the gyro), aim WiFi at faults the robot cannot see itself, and record longer runs with loops before any more learning.

How it was tested

Same runs, same metrics, weights chosen away from the test data

12golden runs in 2 zones (6 west, 6 east)
370WiFi scans, one every ~4 s (~0.8 m)
24APs (an AP = one access point on one band)
5–70 mpath per run (11–90 scans)

Four ways to score a path (all in metres, median over the run, then averaged over the 12 runs)

  • Aligned: the whole estimated path is rotated and shifted to fit the truth as well as possible, then compared. This is what papers report (DPVO, DROID-SLAM). It forgives one global rotation.
  • Causal (added for this report): the same, but each scan's position is taken as it was first estimated, before later scans could correct it. It shows what a robot would know at that moment, apart from the global rotation.
  • Online (from the plan): the rotation is fixed from the first 30 s only. For WiFi alone this turned out to measure mostly which way the map is turned, not tracking (see §1, finding 5).
  • Drift per 5 m: error of the displacement over every 5 m of travel. Needs no global position.

Fairness rules

  • Every weight and threshold was chosen on synthetic sites (24 sites for the solver, 48 runs for the gyro check), never on the 12 real runs.
  • Ceiling rows use the truth (the true AP map, or the true gyro bias). They are limits, not methods.
  • 3 evaluation seeds per method (random AP start positions); learned parts also over 3 trainings. Spreads are in the full table (§5).
  • One exception, labelled where it appears: the odometry weight sweep in §4 was run on the real runs, only to understand a trade-off.
1 · WiFi alone

The new solver ties the best earlier method; the true AP map would gain only 0.27 m

Mean over the 12 runs of each run's median error. Hover a bar for all numbers. The online scale stops at 12 m.

What it shows

  1. A tie. Best hand-weighted variant (radio flow + absolute RSSI + revisits): 1.47 m. Best earlier method: 1.47 m. Absolute RSSI alone in the new solver: 1.46 m. The differences are smaller than the spread over seeds (±0.04 to ±0.13 m).
  2. The AP map is not the bottleneck. Giving the solver the true AP positions and powers only reaches 1.20 m (absolute) and 1.31 m (radio flow). The limit is the RSSI itself: about ±3.5 dB random noise plus ±4.9 dB that depends on the place, the same for ~4 m.
  3. Changes carry less than levels on real data. Radio flow alone (1.59 m) is worse than absolute RSSI alone (1.46 m). The idea was that the change cancels the AP power and the phone gain. It does, but the place-dependent error does not cancel, and it dominates.
  4. Averaging 3 scans helps on synthetic sites and hurts on real ones (2.58 → 2.40 m synthetic, 1.59 → 1.74 m real). It is not the phone repeating cached scans (identical consecutive readings: 11 %, about chance). My best guess: 3 scans span ~1.6 m, less than the ~4 m over which the place error stays the same, so averaging blurs the position without removing that error. Open question.
  5. Online errors of 5–12 m say little about tracking. Applying the same 30 s fit to the final path gives about the same (6.7–8.0 m). In the first 30 s the robot moves only 0.7–5.4 m, and the 3 fixed start positions span less than 0.2 m on 8 of 12 runs. So the map can turn freely: the 30 s fit and the whole-run fit differ by a median of 51–58° for WiFi methods (1° for odometry). The causal error (1.9–2.6 m) is the fairer real-time number.

Weights, chosen on 24 synthetic sites

Each factor type's weight was swept on synthetic sites, and the best was then used unchanged on the real runs. Flow + absolute together: best at 0.03 and 1 (2.02 m).

2 · Recognising places seen before

A learned check ranks better, but most proposed revisits are still wrong

All 12 runs, about 56 proposals in total (one per 6 scans per run, at most one per area). Light bar = within 3 m, dark part = within 2 m.

How many true revisits each run even has

Scan pairs at least 7 scans apart and less than 3 m apart. Runs 1, 3, 6 and 11 have almost none, so any proposal there is wrong. This is why the best possible is 61 %, and why my 60 % gate was nearly impossible.

What it shows

  1. The learned check ranks better. Its ranking score (AUC) is 0.87 vs 0.78 for the raw distance between scans. Over all runs pooled, its top 10 % of true revisits come with 90 % precision vs 51 %.
  2. That gain shrinks once proposals must be spread out. With one proposal per area and a fixed number per run, 32 % are right (synthetic-only model) or 30 % (also trained on the other zone; 32 % when comparing 3-scan sequences), vs 25 % raw.
  3. Training also on the other zone's real runs did not help (AUC 0.83 vs 0.87 synthetic only).
  4. No effect on the path. Radio flow with 3-scan averages: 1.74 m without revisits, 1.68 m with raw revisits, 1.74 m with learned ones. The robust weighting already discounts most wrong links.
3 · The learned update

It learns something real on synthetic sites; on real runs it mostly learns "trust less"

Like DROID-SLAM: after the hand-weighted solve, a small network looks at every radio-flow link (its readings, how well the current path explains it, its neighbours) and proposes a corrected target and a new weight; the solver then re-solves, 12 times. It was trained on synthetic clips for 1,500 steps, 3 times with different seeds (≈30 min on CPU).

Training: learned minus hand-weighted, on the same synthetic clips

Below zero = the network improves on the hand-weighted solve of the same clip. All 3 seeds end about 0.1 m below.

Test on 30 new synthetic sites

Mean 2.19 → 2.00–2.05 m, median 2.02 → 1.53–1.74 m; better on 21, 21 and 25 of 30 sites. So it does learn.

What it does on the real runs

Median weight multiplier per link (×1 = no change). It lowers every weight, long links more (×0.78 at 1 scan apart, ×0.62 at 8). It barely tells true revisits (×0.62) from wrong ones (×0.57). Targets move by only 0.5–1.1 dB.

Results on the real runs

  1. Inside the online loop (2 learned steps after each scan, 12 at the end): 1.455 m, ±0.014 over the 3 trainings. Better than its own hand-weighted start on 8 of 12 runs. Drift 2.19 → 1.97 m per 5 m; causal 2.18 → 2.08 m.
  2. Only at the end (12 steps after the run): 1.52 m, worse than the start (1.47). Almost all of it is run 7 (+0.69 m), the only long run with loops; synthetic training clips have 32 scans.
  3. Same pattern as DPRO v1: large gains on synthetic, small on real, and what transfers is mostly "trust the RSSI less". The simulator still misses something about real RSSI.
4 · With wheel odometry

WiFi as an extra sensor adds nothing; as a check it fixes a broken gyro, but the encoders do it better

Aligned error per run (scale stops at 3 m). Wheel odometry is excellent on 10 runs (0.02–0.32 m) and broken on runs 4 and 9 ("gyro bias").

Step by step

  1. Odometry is far better than WiFi here: 0.48 m vs 1.47 m, and 0.02–0.32 m on 10 of 12 runs. The runs are short (≤70 m), so odometry barely drifts.
  2. Adding radio flow to the odometry changes nothing on average (0.48 → 0.48 m). It helps a little where odometry fails (run 4: 2.55 → 2.38 m) and hurts a little where it is good (run 7: 0.32 → 0.44 m).
  3. More WiFi weight does not rescue the broken runs (first chart below; swept on the real runs, so a diagnostic only). At 30× the tuned weight, runs 4 and 9 improve only from 2.39 m (odometry alone) to 2.03 m on average, while the other 10 runs go from 0.10 to 0.37 m.
  4. Why: the gyro has a constant bias. In runs 4 and 9 the step lengths are right, but the odometry heading turns at a steady wrong rate: −21.9 and +4.6 °/s compared with the truth (residual 2–3°; all other runs under 0.1 °/s). The error is spread over every step, so no single step looks wrong to the solver.
  5. A constant bias is one unknown per run, and WiFi can estimate it. For each candidate bias, the odometry is un-rotated, every AP's model is fitted to that path, and we measure how well the path explains the RSSI. A curled path explains it badly. The best bias is kept only if it fits clearly better than "no bias" (margin 1.3×, chosen on synthetic runs).

Lower = the path explains the RSSI better; dots = best bias found. Run 4: a deep minimum at −22.4 °/s (true −21.9). Run 9: a shallower one at +3.9 (true +4.6). Run 7 (healthy): minimum at 0. Run 1 (healthy, 14 scans): a false minimum at +4.1, not deep enough to pass.

Setting the margin without the real runs

48 synthetic runs of 12–60 scans; half get a bias of 2–25 °/s. With a 1.3× margin: no false correction among the 24 runs without bias (their best was 1.20×), 21 of 24 biased runs corrected, bias error median 0.9 °/s. On our runs: run 4 corrected (1.65×), run 9 not (1.16×), no healthy run touched (at most 1.10×).

Run 4: the raw odometry curls up; after the check it follows the truth. Run 9: the check did not trigger (1.16× is below the 1.3× margin), so its line is the odometry's.

Result

After the run, the WiFi gyro-bias check brings the odometry from 0.48 to 0.30 m on average (drift 0.57 → 0.33 m per 5 m), entirely through run 4 (2.55 → 0.34 m). Removing the true bias would reach 0.11 m, so run 9 is the remaining gap. With a margin of 1.15 (almost as good on synthetic: 0.71 vs 0.69 m) run 9 would also have been fixed (2.23 → 1.10 m) with no false correction on our runs, but changing the margin after seeing the real runs would not be fair, so 1.3 stays.

Not yet in real time

Re-estimating the bias as scans arrive gives no gain yet (causal 0.48 m, same as odometry). On run 4 the bias is spotted after 8 scans (−20 °/s) but the estimate wanders to −28 to −31 °/s mid-run before settling, and a 7 °/s error spins the latest position. On healthy run 8, one spurious trigger at scan 12 moved that scan 6.6 m. A real-time version needs a stability rule (several agreeing scans, a growing margin).

The wheel encoders already see this fault

The robot's /odom heading comes from the IMU gyro (drift −21.86 and +4.54 °/s on runs 4 and 9). Heading from the wheel encoders alone drifts by only −0.14 and −0.11 °/s there. Plain encoder dead reckoning, with no gyro and no WiFi, reaches 0.13 m on average (run 4: 0.09 m, run 9: 0.12 m), better than the WiFi check's 0.30 m. On healthy runs the gyro heading is a little better (run 5: 0.04 vs 0.20 m), so the right odometry baseline is encoders and gyro checked against each other. I found this only after the main runs (dpro/rf/encoder_odometry.py).

6 · What this means

WiFi alone is near its limit; WiFi belongs on faults the robot cannot see itself

  1. On these runs, WiFi alone is close to its limit (~1.2–1.5 m). Four different solvers (DPRO v1, the radio-flow graph, the learned update, the true-AP ceiling) all land between 1.20 and 1.53 m. More modelling of the same signal is unlikely to move this much.
  2. Fix the odometry baseline first (≈0.5 day): integrate the wheel encoders, check their heading against the gyro, and use that as the odometry row (encoders alone: 0.13 m).
  3. Aim WiFi at faults the robot cannot see itself (≈2 days): wheel slip, being lifted or carried, or encoders and gyro disagreeing with no way to tell which is right; made real-time with a stability rule, using the same "how well does this path explain the RSSI" test.
  4. Record longer runs with loops (robot time): our runs are 5–70 m with few revisits, too short for odometry to drift and too few loops for revisits to matter. That is where WiFi should help most.
  5. Fix the simulator before more learning: both networks (v1 and this one) learn real gains on synthetic sites that mostly vanish on real runs, and 3-scan averaging behaves differently in the two worlds.
7 · Honest notes

What to keep in mind when reading these numbers

  • Two of my gates were badly set. Phase 2's 60 % was nearly impossible (best possible 61 %). Phase 4 assumed long runs where odometry drifts; ours are short.
  • The online metric from the plan mostly measures the map's rotation for WiFi-only methods. I kept it and added the causal metric.
  • The gyro fault was found by looking at the truth for runs 4 and 9. The fix uses no truth and its margin was set on synthetic runs, but only 2 real runs have the fault: far too few to claim a detection rate.
  • The phase 4 baseline was too weak. I compared WiFi against /odom, whose heading is the biased gyro, not against the wheel encoders, which reach 0.13 m on their own. The WiFi gyro-bias check stands as evidence that WiFi carries heading information, not as the best fix for this robot.
  • Small data: 12 short runs in 2 zones of one building, 370 scans. Differences under ~0.1 m between methods are within the seed spread.
  • DPRO v1 is 1.53 m here and 1.51 m in the notebook: same code and trainings, different evaluation seeds.
  • Aligned errors forgive one global rotation (as in the literature). The causal and drift numbers do not depend on that, and they tell the same story.

Code, commands, outputs

python -m dpro.rf.tune --out rf_tune.json                       # phase 1 weights, 24 synthetic sites (~10 min)
python -m dpro.rf.tune_extra --tune rf_tune.json                # wider grid where the best sat on the edge
bash tools/notebook_runs/train_rf_revisit.sh                    # phase 2: learned revisit check, 3 variants
python -m dpro.rf.eval_revisit --models /root/navlori/runs/dpro/revisit --out revisit_eval.json
bash tools/notebook_runs/train_rf_net.sh                        # phase 3: 3 trainings in parallel (~30 min)
python -m dpro.rf.odometry --tune rf_tune.json --out rf_tune_odo.json   # phase 4 weights (synthetic)
python -m dpro.rf.odo_diag --out results/odo_diag.json          # phase 4 weight diagnostic (real runs, not a method)
python -m dpro.rf.gyro_bias --out results/gyro_bias.json        # gyro check: margin on synthetic, estimates on real (~2 min)
bash tools/notebook_runs/eval_rf.sh rf|v1|ctx|rev|odo           # every method on the 12 runs, 3 seeds
bash tools/notebook_runs/eval_rf_phase3.sh                      # learned rows, gyro rows, synthetic test, behaviour
